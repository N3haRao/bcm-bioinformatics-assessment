RNA-seq Differential Expression Analysis: BCM-119 Treatment in A549 Cells
Internal analysis notes, prepared for the bioinformatics core

STUDY OVERVIEW

This experiment tested the transcriptional effect of Compound BCM-119, an
experimental EGFR/MAPK pathway inhibitor, on A549 cells, a human lung
adenocarcinoma cell line. Cells were treated with either 10 uM BCM-119 or a
DMSO vehicle control for 48 hours, with 3 biological replicates per group
(6 samples total).

METHODS

Total RNA was extracted with the Qiagen RNeasy Mini Kit. Libraries were
prepared with the NEBNext Ultra II Directional RNA Library Prep Kit and
sequenced on an Illumina NovaSeq 6000, paired-end 100 bp, targeting 30
million read pairs per sample. Reads were aligned to the GRCh38 primary
assembly using the GENCODE v33 annotation (the same annotation build used
elsewhere in this project) with STAR, and gene-level counts were quantified
with featureCounts. Differential expression was called with DESeq2 using
the default Wald test and Benjamini-Hochberg correction for multiple testing.

SIGNIFICANCE CRITERIA

A gene was called significant for this study if BOTH of the following held:
adjusted p-value (padj) < 0.05, AND absolute log2 fold change > 0.5. Genes
meeting only one of the two criteria are not counted as significant here,
even if they are highlighted in the results table for context.

The full differential expression analysis covered every gene in the GENCODE
v33 annotation. This summary table is a curated panel of 28 genes of
particular interest for this project: canonical EGFR/MAPK/PI3K-AKT pathway
members, well known oncogenes and tumor suppressors, two standard reference
genes, and a handful of tissue-irrelevant genes included as a sanity check.

REFERENCE GENES

GAPDH and ACTB were included in the panel as standard reference
("housekeeping") genes. Both showed no significant change between conditions
(padj well above 0.05 for each), which supports the normalization used
across the two groups.

A NOTE ON THE TISSUE-IRRELEVANT GENES

INS (insulin, normally expressed in pancreatic beta cells) and HBB (beta
globin, normally expressed in erythroid cells) were included specifically
because they are not expected to be expressed at meaningful levels in a lung
epithelial cell line. Their very low baseMean values in the results table
are the expected outcome, not a technical failure, and served as an
additional sanity check that the library preparation was tissue-appropriate.

RESULT INTERPRETATION

MYC showed the largest magnitude change of any gene in the panel, a strong
and highly significant downregulation. This is consistent with MYC's role as
a transcriptional driver of proliferation downstream of EGFR/MAPK signaling,
and its loss is expected when that upstream pathway is inhibited. CDK4 was
also significantly downregulated, consistent with reduced cell cycle
progression, while CDKN2A (p16) was significantly upregulated, consistent
with the cell cycle arrest response that typically accompanies reduced CDK4
activity. IL6 was significantly upregulated, which is consistent with a
mild inflammatory or cellular stress response to compound treatment rather
than a direct pathway effect. EGFR itself showed a modest but significant
increase, which is a commonly reported compensatory feedback response when
its own downstream signaling is blocked.

WHAT THIS ANALYSIS DOES NOT COVER

Functional validation of these transcriptional changes by qPCR or western
blot has not yet been performed and is planned as a follow-up. This analysis
also does not include any dose-response data; only the single 10 uM
concentration described above was tested in this experiment.
